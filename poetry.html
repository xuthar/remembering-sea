The book of

Words written for the water, and for everything it could not wash away.

01 — 08 / EIGHT LETTERS TO THE SEA

Not every departure
is a goodbye.

Eight original poems about longing and distance, about afternoons already gone and the places that continue to live inside us. Read them slowly, as if you were standing at the shore.

I / THE SHORE THAT STAYED

The Shore That Stayed

I returned when the beaches were empty, when the light had forgotten the sand. The water said nothing about leaving. It simply reached for my hand. There were footsteps I could not recover, and a name I no longer could say. The waves knew the shape of that silence; they carried its edges away. I stood where the afternoon ended, where the cold settled softly on stone. The shore had not waited to find me. And still, I was not quite alone.

MEMORY / RETURN
II / A MAP MADE OF SALT

Where the Water Forgets

There is no monument beneath the foam, no inscription for the vanished years. The sea erases every road toward home, then leaves us with the salt of tears. I once believed that water could remember the weight of all the things we lost. But every wave unlearns the last September, and every crossing has its cost. Perhaps forgetting is its tender art: to hold a grief, then set it free. I wish the tide could teach my heart the strange, unfinished mercy of the sea.

ABSENCE / MERCY
III / THE HOURS BETWEEN

Low Tide

At low tide the stones begin to speak, showing their wounds to the open sky. A little world lies bare and weak, where all the buried seasons lie. I see the things I tried to hide: a rusted hope, a fragile vow, the shadow walking at my side, a life that once was only now. The water will return at dusk, and cover every mark it made. But I have learned that even trust can live beneath the things that fade.

TIME / REVELATION
IV / THE UNWRITTEN LETTER

To the Far Horizon

I send no letter to the distance. There is no address in the blue. Only a gull against the weather, and everything I never knew. The horizon keeps its ancient promise: to look like somewhere I could go. Yet every mile of restless water makes more of what I do not know. If one day I should reach its border, if all the endless blue should cease, I hope to find beyond that silence not an answer — only peace.

DISTANCE / HOPE
V / WINTER COAST

Winter at the Sea

The winter shore has fewer voices. The empty benches face the rain. A shutter beats against a window; the wind repeats a small refrain. No bright umbrellas mark the distance, no footprints hurry to the foam. The sea has shed its summer colours and found another way to be home. I love it most when it is lonely, when clouds and water lose their seam, when even daylight seems to whisper of all we were, or wished to dream.

WINTER / SOLITUDE
VI / A LIGHT THAT REMAINS

The Last Lighthouse

It stands where all the roads have ended, a small defiance in the rain. And night after night, with nothing promised, it throws its light across the plain. No ship is certain to discover the narrow comfort of its beam. Still it searches through the weather, faithful to an absent dream. I think of all the hearts like lighthouses: awake for someone far from shore, keeping a little light from dying, though they are needed there no more.

DEVOTION / DARKNESS
VII / WHAT REMAINS

Driftwood

A branch arrives without a story, its leaves surrendered long ago. It has survived the tide's indifference, the patient violence of the flow. I lift it from the morning shallows: a crooked shape, a salt-white scar. How strange that something so forgotten could find its way from somewhere far. We too are carried, changed, and weathered; we too are strangers to the land. And sometimes being found is simply resting a moment in a hand.

CHANGE / SURVIVAL
VIII / THE RETURN

When the Water Is Still

At last the wind releases evening. The sea grows quiet, dark and wide. Beyond the harbour, lights are trembling, like little stars that learned to hide. There is no ending in this silence, no final word for what has been. The waves return without a reason, and wash the world to blue again. And I, who came to seek an answer, find only water, dusk and air — but something in their gentle absence has made it easier to bear.

ACCEPTANCE / STILLNESS

“The ocean is not an answer.
It is a place to ask.”
